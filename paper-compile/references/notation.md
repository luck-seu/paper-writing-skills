# Template: notation.md (machine view)

Notation table for writing consistency — **as used in the paper**, not
as declared: commands.tex defines the global supply, the view records
what the prose actually consumes. commands.tex is a shared macro
library inherited across papers, so unused declarations are expected
and are NOT listed (author ruling 2026-10-25) — the actionable signal
is the duplicate section below: you hand-typed what a declared macro
already prints.

**Verdict: script-perfect.** The scan (math-mode control sequences),
counting, first-section lookup, and the duplicate check (string
equality between a hand-typed token and a declared expansion) are
mechanical. What a symbol *denotes*, and whether the notation is the
right choice, is judgment and lives with the author (and the target
repo's style guide when present), not here.

Rows are in first-appearance order. "hand-typed" rows with no
declared macro are the notation-without-a-macro signal: if the symbol
recurs, the fix is a macro in commands.tex (author-ruled file), not
more hand-typing.

## Source & regeneration

- Math segments: inline `$...$`, `\(...\)`, one-line `\[...\]`, and
  the interior of display environments (`equation`, `align`,
  `gather`, `eqnarray`, `displaymath`, `math`, `*` variants) over
  the prose files (a `sections/` tree or flat root `.tex`);
  comment-stripped.
- Tokens: every control sequence plus one simple braced argument
  (`\mathcal{X}`, `\card{\A}`); structural commands (begin/end,
  cite/ref/label, text, frac, spacing) are skipped by an ignore
  list.
- Macro mapping: commands.tex Math zone (name → expansion).

Known scan limits (documented in the view footer): bare letters
(variables with no control sequence) are not tokenized; a token
swallows its own argument; one-line `\[...\]` and begin/end lines
inside a display environment are approximations.

`sh scripts/compile.sh notation`.
